Pole guide

Choosing the right pole

A quick, general overview of the three pole types we make.

PSCC pole

Prestressed cement concrete. High-tensile steel wires are tensioned before the concrete sets, which makes the pole stronger and stiffer for its weight than a reinforced pole.

  • Solid, tapered section
  • Lighter for the same load
  • Low maintenance

RCC pole

Reinforced cement concrete. Steel reinforcement bars are cast into the concrete without prestressing. A simple, widely used option for lighter line loads.

  • Steel-bar reinforcement
  • Suited to lighter loads
  • Straightforward to handle

PSCC spun pole

A hollow, round, tapered prestressed pole made by spinning the mould, which compacts the concrete. The round section is equally strong in every direction.

  • Hollow, circular section
  • Dense, compacted concrete
  • Highest working load we make

What is “working load”?

The working load is the sideways force the pole is designed to carry in normal service, such as wind on the pole and the pull of the conductors. A higher working load suits longer spans, heavier conductors and angle or end locations.

Where concrete poles are used

  • LT distribution lines
  • HT distribution lines
  • Street lighting
  • Rural electrification
  • Utility & turnkey works
